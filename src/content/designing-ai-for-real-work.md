# Designing AI for Real Work

**Two internal AI experiences focused on trust, clarity, and making complex information actionable.**

**Company:** Ampersand  
**Role:** Product Designer, collaborating with Engineering  
**Focus:** AI Product Design · Conversational Interfaces · Human-AI Interaction · Data Visualization

## Overview

AI introduces new possibilities for how people interact with software, but it also introduces new design challenges. When an interface can generate answers, interpret requests, and present information dynamically, how do we help users understand its capabilities, verify its outputs, and know when to trust it?

At Ampersand, I collaborated with engineers on two internal AI tools designed to address different business needs:

1. **AI Knowledge Assistant:** Helping account executives find reliable answers from internal company documents.
2. **Campaign Data Agent:** Helping internal users explore campaign and viewership information through natural-language queries and structured visualizations.

While the use cases were different, both required thinking beyond a traditional chat interface and considering how AI could support real workflows.

---

## Project 01 — AI Knowledge Assistant

### Making AI-generated answers easier to verify

**The Problem**

Account executives need access to accurate company information to support their daily work. An AI-powered assistant could make retrieving that information more conversational, but simply producing an answer wasn't enough.

The experience also needed to address a fundamental challenge: **How can users determine whether an AI-generated answer is reliable?**

### The Experience

We designed an internal AI assistant that allows account executives to ask questions and receive responses grounded in company documents.

Three capabilities were central to the experience:

**1. Grounding answers in internal documents**

Rather than treating the assistant as a general-purpose chatbot, the experience was designed around answering questions using company documentation.

*Why it matters:* Users need information relevant to their organization's actual policies, processes, and knowledge.

**2. Making answers verifiable through citations**

Responses include references to the source documents, giving users a way to investigate where information originated.

*Why it matters:* A confident-sounding answer isn't necessarily a correct one. Source visibility helps users evaluate information rather than accepting it blindly.

**3. Communicating when the AI doesn't know**

The assistant acknowledges when it cannot provide an answer instead of presenting unsupported information as fact.

*Why it matters:* Communicating uncertainty is as important as communicating an answer. It helps establish realistic expectations about the system's capabilities.

### The Design Principle

**Trust should come from transparency, not just a polished interface.**

This project challenged me to think about AI interactions differently. In traditional software, designers can often anticipate and define what the interface will display. With generative AI, the content is dynamic, and the experience must account for uncertainty.

Designing trustworthy AI means helping users evaluate its responses, not simply making those responses appear credible.

## Project 02 — Campaign & Viewership Data Agent

### Making complex data accessible through natural language

**The Problem**

Campaign and viewership information can be complex to explore, especially when users need to find specific insights across data-heavy workflows.

Conversational AI offered another way to interact with this information. However, a chat interface alone wouldn't necessarily make the results easier to understand or use.

The design challenge was: **How might we combine the flexibility of natural language with the clarity of structured data interfaces?**

### The Experience

I collaborated with engineers on an interface for an internal AI agent that allows users to query campaign and viewership data through natural language.

Rather than relying exclusively on text responses, the experience brings together conversational interaction, data visualization, and shortcuts for common functions.

### Key Design Considerations

**1. Natural language as an entry point**

Users can ask questions about campaign and viewership information conversationally.

*Why it matters:* This provides an alternative to navigating multiple controls or working through complex data queries.

**2. Presenting information in the format that best supports the task**

The agent can return structured tables and charts instead of limiting every response to text.

*Why it matters:* Different information requires different representations. Tables support detailed comparisons, while charts can make patterns and trends easier to understand.

**3. Moving beyond a chat-only experience**

The interface includes shortcut functions that provide access to capabilities beyond typing a message.

*Why it matters:* Conversational interaction is flexible, but it isn't necessarily the most efficient interaction for every task. Purpose-built controls can complement chat and make common workflows more accessible.

### The Design Principle

**Not every AI interaction should be a conversation.**

One of the most interesting aspects of this project was exploring how conversational interfaces and traditional UI patterns could work together.

Rather than replacing existing interaction models with chat, the opportunity was to use AI where it simplified access to information while retaining structured interfaces where they offered greater clarity.

## What These Projects Taught Me

Working on these two AI experiences reinforced three principles that continue to shape my approach to AI product design.

### Make trust actionable

Trust isn't something users should be asked to give an AI system automatically. Interfaces should provide the information people need to evaluate responses, understand limitations, and exercise judgment.

### Design around the task, not the technology

Adding a conversational interface doesn't automatically improve a product. The right interaction depends on what the user is trying to accomplish and how the resulting information will be used.

### Keep people informed and in control

AI can reduce the effort required to access information, but design still has an important role in making its behavior understandable and its outputs usable.

## Looking Ahead

These projects expanded my perspective from designing predictable, rule-based interfaces to exploring experiences where content and responses are generated dynamically.

They also strengthened my interest in human-AI interaction—particularly how we design systems that are capable and efficient while remaining understandable, verifiable, and useful.

**The question I continue to explore is not simply what AI can do, but how we design experiences that help people use its capabilities with confidence and good judgment.**
